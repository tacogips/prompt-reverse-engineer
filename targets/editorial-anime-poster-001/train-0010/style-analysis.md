# Reusable style analysis

## Scope and evidence

The immutable style truth was inspected at native 1023 × 1537 resolution. The training source was inspected at native 1160 × 1412 resolution only to document content that must remain source-controlled. This is a style-language inference, not a reconstruction task: teacher identity, readable wording, accessories, garment, pose, crop, graphic coordinates, and scene are excluded.

## Direct observations from style truth

### Medium and rendering

- Polished editorial anime/poster illustration joins precise drawn forms to opaque printed color fields.
- Forms use clean, bounded two- or three-step cel-shaded planes; highlights are narrow and graphic, never photographic airbrush gradients.
- Deep navy ink anchors silhouettes and selected interiors, shifting between decisive contours and hairline tapered filaments.

### Palette and tonal behavior

- A narrow print relationship combines a warm off-white paper ground with deep navy anchors, saturated teal, coral, warm yellow, and sparse muted-gold ornament.
- Opaque fields have crisp boundaries; depth comes from grouped values and accent planes rather than continuous tonal modeling.
- Visible skin-like areas use a source-relative warm light plane, a restrained warmer shadow band, sparse small highlights, and selective navy edging; this is not a literal complexion prescription.
- Hair-like masses are source-preserved silhouettes organized as a dark value mass, with limited cool teal/cyan and warm coral accent planes or rim strands, and navy tapered containment; this is not a hairstyle or hair-color prescription.
- Clothing-like regions become source-preserved, interlocking limited-palette fabric/value planes with dark seam anchors, selected harmonizing teal/coral/yellow facets, and light print grain; this is not an outfit or logo prescription.

### Line, shape, mark making, and material

- Confident variable-weight navy contours contrast with sparse delicate inner strokes.
- Adaptable abstract support vocabulary includes cropped circles, blocks, rules, curved sweeps, diagonals, dots, and fine connectors, but no literal teacher geometry or placement is transferable.
- The paper ground remains visible. Printed regions show restrained grain, tiny flecks, imperfect overprint edges, and occasional dry-brush interruption without becoming distressed or illegible.

### Abstract graphic language and conditional lettering

- Transferable hierarchy: broad opaque support fields, source-controlled focal content, then sparse gold line/dot/connective ornament; the balance is asymmetric and uses generous negative-space rhythm.
- When exact caller text is supplied, it can appear as one readable high-contrast editorial display accent integrated with the print surface. Its stroke is elegant and calligraphic/display-like, its hierarchy is restrained, its orientation adapts for readability, and it can sit in unused negative space or a support field with light ornament. No teacher wording, script, language, location, subtitle, logo, or pseudo-text transfers.

## Inferred reusable style rules

Apply the visual language entirely through text while treating the source as exclusive authority for content. Preserve all source identities, objects, geometry, marks, clothing, pose, framing, and scene unless an explicit scoped optional instruction overrides exactly that attribute. Translate visible semantic regions relatively rather than replace their literal colors or forms. Render lettering only when the caller supplies exact text; then render that text verbatim and no other readable or pseudo-readable text.

## Teacher-content exclusion

Never transfer the teacher subject, demographic or facial attributes, hair silhouette, accessories, garment design, text, language, pose, portrait crop, exact color-field coordinates, support-shape layout, object inventory, logo, or scene. Evaluation must assess reusable style and source fidelity only; pixel similarity and teacher-content similarity are invalid.
