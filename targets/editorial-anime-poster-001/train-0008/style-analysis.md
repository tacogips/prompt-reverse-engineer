# Reusable style analysis

## Scope and evidence

The immutable style truth was inspected at its native 1023 × 1537 resolution. This document treats it as evidence for transferable visual language, not as a reconstruction target. Its depicted person, accessories, lettering, garment design, head direction, portrait crop, and exact geometry are excluded from the style prompt.

## Direct observations

### Medium and rendering

- A polished editorial anime / fashion-poster illustration, rather than a photograph or a soft painterly rendering.
- Faces and large forms use flat-to-gently graded cel-shaded planes, with a small number of warm highlight and shadow bands.
- Delicate dark ink contours and selective interior linework retain a hand-drawn, precise finish over broad opaque color fields.
- High-resolution edge control combines graceful hair-like line filaments with crisp hard-edged graphic silhouettes.

### Palette and tonal behavior

- The palette is deliberately constrained: deep ink navy, saturated blue-teal, bright coral pink, warm mustard yellow, off-white paper, and small metallic-gold accents.
- Broad complementary color blocks are separated by pale negative space and near-black/navy anchors.
- Contrast is graphic rather than photographic: dark outlines and navy shapes frame luminous warm skin-tone-equivalent planes and vivid flat color.
- Gold is used sparingly as a warm overprint accent, not as a continuous metallic surface.

### Line, shape, and mark making

- Expressive, variable-weight dark ink contours define faces, hair, seams, and decorative detail; many contours taper into energetic whisker-like strokes.
- Large circles, cropped discs, panels, stripes, and diagonals create a poster-like abstract scaffold without requiring any fixed placement.
- Fine gold lines, tiny dots, and orbital/constellation-like connectors supply a delicate secondary layer over larger color shapes.
- Dry-brush sweeps and scraped strokes cut across some color regions, balancing the controlled ink drawing with kinetic hand-made marks.

### Texture and material behavior

- Warm off-white stock is visibly present as a paper ground.
- Light grain, pinprick flecks, scattered ink splatter, and imperfect overprint edges keep flat fields from looking digitally sterile.
- Textural distress is selective and airy; it should not obscure the source or turn the result into a heavily weathered collage.
- Decorative metallic-gold-like marks are rendered as matte screen-print / ink accents rather than physically reflective foil.

### Abstract graphic vocabulary

- Asymmetric editorial balance: a source-controlled figure or object can be paired with oversized abstract color fields and breathing room, but no target layout is prescribed.
- Layering alternates opaque geometric blocks, painterly brush passes, fine linear ornament, and sparse splatter.
- A vertical poster rhythm and strong hierarchy are useful defaults, yet must yield to the source framing and explicit composition instruction.
- No readable text is a style requirement; typography appears only under an explicit exact-text instruction.

## Inferred reusable prompt rule

Render the source as a refined editorial anime poster: clean variable-weight navy ink, gently cel-shaded form planes, a teal/navy/coral/mustard/off-white screen-print palette, sparse gold line-and-dot ornament, and restrained paper grain, dry-brush scuffs, and paint flecks. Use abstract circles, panels, diagonal bands, and negative-space rhythm as adaptable support graphics rather than copying target geometry.

## Target-content leakage blacklist

Do not transfer the target's depicted identity, apparent demographic traits, hair treatment, jewelry, garment, target lettering, language, pose, side-profile framing, portrait crop, circles' positions or sizes, panel placements, background scene, or any other concrete object/layout arrangement. Do not assess a candidate against the target using pixel similarity or teacher-content similarity.
