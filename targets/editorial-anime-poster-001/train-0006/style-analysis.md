# Style Analysis

## Inspection record

- Inspected `truth/style-reference.png` locally at its original 1023 × 1537 PNG resolution.
- This analysis treats the image as a style-only reference. Its depicted individual, wardrobe, accessories, pose-specific features, and readable writing are excluded from transferable content.

## Direct observations

- Portrait-oriented editorial illustration with a single large bust/shoulder figure and generous cream negative space.
- Crisp, dark navy, variable-width ink contours define facial features, hair, garment boundaries, and graphic accents; facial shading is made of broad, flat warm planes rather than photographic gradients.
- The palette is organized around deep navy, cyan/teal, coral-pink, saturated mustard yellow, cream, and restrained metallic-gold/tan accents.
- Large overlapping circular color fields, narrow vertical rules, rectangular navy blocks, and diagonal dry-brush sweeps create a layered poster grid.
- The treatment combines flat cel-like color blocking with energetic fine linework, small paint flecks/splatters, dry-brush abrasion, and a faint printed-paper/grain texture.
- The figure is framed prominently and has upward directional energy. Abstract fields carry the visual movement instead of a naturalistic setting.
- Readable lettering and small decorative marks appear in the reference; they are non-transferable unless an exact-text instruction is explicitly supplied.

## Transferable style policy

- Transfer the editorial illustrated medium, high-contrast palette relationships, ink-line quality, abstract graphic vocabulary, print texture, and energetic framing language.
- Infer output framing and proportion from the current style reference at generation time. Treat an explicit composition instruction as higher priority.
- Preserve source-controlled content from the source reference; do not substitute the style reference's person, clothing, accessories, or writing.

## Inference and uncertainty

- The artwork appears intentionally designed as a print/poster composition rather than a literal scene; this is an interpretation supported by the graphic fields and surface treatment.
- Some marks may be simulated brush texture rather than scanned analog material. The policy therefore calls for a subtle print/brush texture rather than claiming a specific physical process.
